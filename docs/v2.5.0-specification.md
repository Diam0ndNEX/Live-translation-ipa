## 1. What changed for the person using the app
- **Two modes: Legacy and Low Latency.** Hybrid Recovery is gone from Translate (two chips), Settings, the settings search, Experimental features, Diagnostics, test runs and reports.
- **A saved Hybrid choice** (setting, backup or preset) becomes Low Latency once. Translate then shows one notice, "Hybrid Recovery was removed". Diagnostics reports the migration reason `migrated_hybrid_low_latency`.
- **Low Latency without its language pack**: Start capture does not open the system sheet. A question appears: "Low Latency needs a language pack", naming the pair, with Download (only when the pack can be downloaded), Use Legacy and Cancel. The app never changes the model by itself; the old Hybrid fallback to Apple Default no longer exists.
- **Old runs** stay in the list as "Hybrid (removed)". They are not compared and are left out of combined reports (the report says how many were left out).
- **Seven new switches**, all ON, in Experimental features under "New in 2.5.0" (section 3).

Kept on purpose:

- `TranslationSystem` decodes an unknown or removed name as Low Latency so old warm-up records still decode; warm-up records of a Hybrid run are dropped, and Hybrid runs are recognised by their own stored mode name.
- Folder and script names `Tests/ThreeModes`, `scripts/test_three_modes.py`, `compare_three_modes.py`: `build.yml` names them and cannot be pushed from chat.
- The stored measurement-boundary sentence still contains the words "Hybrid/Low Latency". It is compared as text between runs; changing it would withhold every comparison between 2.4.0 and 2.5.0 runs.
- Generic Apple Default plumbing inside `ModeTranslationService` (hosted iOS 18 path, lease). Nothing reaches it in Low Latency. Left to keep this change smaller; it can go in a later cleanup.
Known small flaw: the migration reason stays `migrated_hybrid_low_latency` after the person later picks a mode by hand.

### 3.1 Item E, trusted caption band (Low Latency only)
Until 2.4.0 a focused read whose text had changed was always thrown away and the whole area was read again, so the first read of every new caption was a whole-area read. Now: after two whole-area reads in a row whose text lay inside one band (caption lines plus 1.6 line heights above and below, at least 5% of the screen), focused reads cover that band (also in the gap between two captions; the whole area is still read every 0.75 s), and a changed caption read there is used directly when every line has confidence 0.85 or more and no line comes within 0.35 line heights of the band edge.
Trade-off against the 2.4.0 limits (coverage): text that appears outside the band is not seen until the next whole-area read, at most 0.75 s later. A caption that grows by more than one line can be read cut short until then. A whole-area read that finds text outside the band ends the trust.
Deviations from the plan, by my judgement:
- **Not built: OCR from the pixel buffer with a region of interest.** Today the image is scaled down before OCR. A region of interest on the raw buffer would be read at full resolution, about four times the pixels for a caption band, so it could be slower, and it needs a different rotation path that cannot be checked without a device. A silent failure there would mean no subtitles on every phone.
- **Not in Legacy.** Legacy never had focused reads, and it is the mode the pack question offers as the safe choice. Its reading is unchanged by E.

### 3.2 Item F, Legacy head start
On a read that was started by a screen change (or when no caption is being watched), the extension first runs a fast OCR pass on the same image. Its text can be sent as an early packet with its own identity (`...:hs:n`), so Legacy starts the Apple Default call while the normal read is still running. The fast pass never goes through the confirmation rule and can never confirm or show anything; the normal read and the pixel check decide that exactly as in 2.4.0.
"Not wasted" is enforced by a score (`HeadStartGovernor`): each fast pass is later marked right (the confirmed caption had exactly that text) or wrong (other text confirmed, or replaced before confirmation). The head start is sent only while at least 80% of the last 12 were right, after at least 6. While it is off, the fast pass runs once in six reads to keep the score current. It is skipped under pressure, with Fast OCR on, with contrast boost "always", and for languages the fast recogniser does not support.
Cost when it is wrong: a wasted Apple Default call that must be cancelled before the confirmed one (the overlap the 2.4.0 audit described). Cost always: the fast pass itself delays the normal read by its duration (measured as "fast pass P50/P95").
The 2.4.0 deferral (no early call for an armed first sighting) is unchanged and still applies to the normal read's first-sighting packet.
